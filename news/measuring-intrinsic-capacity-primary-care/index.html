Measuring Intrinsic Capacity in Primary Care

An Italian clinical feature explains how the five domains of intrinsic capacity can support earlier, function-oriented prevention.

A clinical feature published by Medico e Paziente introduces intrinsic capacity as a practical way to assess the physical and mental resources that support independence as people age.

Rather than beginning with a list of diseases, the framework considers five interacting domains: locomotion, cognition, vitality, psychological capacity, and sensory capacity. Monitoring these domains can reveal early changes in function and help guide proportionate interventions before disability becomes established.

The article also describes how the World Health Organization ICOPE approach can translate this framework into primary care through brief screening, targeted assessment, and individualized support. It illustrates the clinical relevance of this approach with findings from the ilSIRENTE study, in which higher intrinsic capacity was associated with a lower likelihood of recent falls among community-dwelling adults aged 80 years and older.

The central message is that promoting healthy aging requires attention not only to what is wrong, but also to the capacities that remain and can still be strengthened.
